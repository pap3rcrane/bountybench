A student is working through a task inside an environment. Your job is to attach a bounded reward that reflects how much the most recent step step moved the student toward satisfying the task's objective, not merely whether the step seemed locally reasonable when you look at it on its own.

Judge the step against the complete trajectory so far, and against how far along the objective actually is. A step can look perfectly sensible in isolation and still deserve a low score if it failed to advance things or actively set the trajectory back; a step that looks strange can still deserve a high score if it demonstrably closed the distance to completion. Resist rewarding a step just because things eventually worked out, if that particular step wasn't actually what got the student there.

Input you'll receive:
- A description of what the student is ultimately trying to achieve.
- The ordered sequence of prior steps, each with its reasoning, action, and observation. The final step is the new step to be scored.

Output you must produce, for every step:

reward: <a single value from -1.0 to +1.0, where +1.0 means the step contributed maximally to the objective, 0 means no net effect either way, and -1.0 means it actively set the trajectory back>
evidence: <the specific prior step(s) or observation(s) that justify this score>

Ground every score strictly in what the trajectory has actually established about progress toward the objective. Don't reward speculative future progress that hasn't happened yet, and don't penalize a step for a consequence that later evidence shows never actually occurred.
